GATHER
PHASE 1
PERPLEXITY
Perplexity is search-first and always cites sources.
Every answer it produces links to the original source material. That citation habit is what
makes it the right tool for phase one. You're not looking for AI opinion. You are looking
for sourced, traceable starting material.
Start every research session here.
What to use Perplexity for:
Source discovery. Web research. Market data. Current events. Regulatory updates.
Competitor news. Any question where you need a sourced answer rather than a
generated one.
How to prompt Perplexity for better research:
Don't ask general questions. Ask specific ones.
Instead of: "What are the trends in AI hiring?" 
Ask: "What do 2026 employer surveys report about AI skill requirements in job
postings? Cite sources."
Instead of: "Tell me about competitor X." 
Ask: "What has competitor X announced in the last 90 days? What do their recent job
postings suggest about their strategic direction? Cite all sources."
The more specific the question, the more useful the sourced answer.
TOOL:
GROOK
What Grok adds to phase one:
For anything where real-time intelligence matters, add Grok. Grok is connected directly
to X data in real-time. News breaks there before it reaches any newsletter. If you're
tracking a fast-moving market, a competitor's announcement, or a regulatory
development, Grok surfaces what Perplexity may not yet have indexed. Use Perplexity
for structured, sourced research. Use Grok when you need to know what's happening
right now.
TOOL:
Phase one output: A set of sourced notes, links, and data points. Not polished. Not
synthesized. Just raw material you can verify and hand to phase two.

CLAUDE
Claude is built for long-form thinking. It reads large amounts of material, identifies
patterns, synthesizes across sources, and extracts what matters.
This is where your phase one material becomes insight.
What to use Claude for:
Summarizing findings. Identifying patterns. Synthesizing across multiple sources.
Extracting key points. Generating strategic takeaways. Pressure-testing your
interpretation.

THE THREE MISTAKES MOST PEOPLE MAKE IN
PHASE TWO:
MISTAKE 1: 
TRUSTING FIRST OUTPUTS. 
The first synthesis is a starting point, not a conclusion. Ask Claude to identify
the weakest assumption in its own analysis. The follow-up almost always
produces something more valuable than the initial response.
MISTAKE 2: 
WEAK PROMPTING.
Generic input produces generic output. The prompts above include specific
output requirements, number of findings, format, confidence levels. That
specificity is what separates useful synthesis from a summary you would
have written yourself.
MISTAKE 3: 
SHALLOW SOURCING. 
If phase one did not produce real sources, phase two cannot fix that. The
quality of analysis is directly proportional to the quality of the inputs.
HOW TO VERIFY WHAT PHASE TWO PRODUCES:
Before any research finding becomes a claim you act on or share, verify it.
The AI Report covered this directly: cross-verify significant claims across two or
more models. 
If Claude synthesizes a key finding, paste the same underlying data into a
second model and compare. 
Agreement across models is not proof of accuracy, but disagreement is a signal
to dig further.
For factual claims, go back to the original sourced links from phase one. 
Verify the claim in the primary source.
Never share AI-produced research with a senior stakeholder without having
traced at least the key facts to a named, verifiable source.

PRO TIP FROM THE EXECUTIVE PROMPT LIBRARY: 
Phase three output formats:
The same phase two synthesis can produce different outputs for different
audiences. The research does not change. The framing does.
For a CEO: three findings and one recommendation. For a board: risk context
and financial implications. For a client: how it affects them specifically.
For a team: what it means for how they work next week.
Tell the model who it is writing for and what that person cares most about. The
output adjusts accordingly.
