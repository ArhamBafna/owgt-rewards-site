CATEGORY 1
Decisions with long time horizons, significant resource implications,
and outcomes that are hard to reverse.
1. Pressure-Test a Strategy
"Act as a rigorous strategic critic. Here is my strategy or plan:
[describe]. Identify every assumption embedded in it, stated and
unstated. For each assumption, rate it on two dimensions: how
confident we should be that it's true, and how much damage it would
do to the strategy if it turned out to be wrong. Flag the two or
three assumptions that pose the highest combined risk."

3. Decide Whether to Pivot or Stay the Course
"I need to decide whether to pivot or stay the course on [describe
current direction]. Here is what the data is telling me: [share
what you know]. Here is what I'm uncertain about: [describe]. Build
two scenarios: one where the current signals are a real problem
requiring a change, and one where they are noise that will resolve.
Tell me what I would need to see in the next 60 days to know which
scenario is true."
4. Assess an Acquisition or Partnership
"I'm considering [acquisition/partnership] with [describe the
opportunity]. Evaluate it across: strategic fit with our current
direction, capabilities we gain, risks we absorb, integration
complexity, and what it signals to the market. Give me the three
questions I need to have answered in due diligence before I can
make this decision."
5. Prioritize Between Competing Initiatives
"Here are the strategic initiatives I'm choosing between: [list].
Prioritize them using these criteria: expected impact on revenue
or competitive position, resource requirements, time to first
results, and risk if we deprioritize. Give me a ranked order with
the one condition that would cause you to reorder the top two."

When something isn't working and you need to identify what's

6. Root Cause Analysis
"Here is a problem I keep seeing: [describe]. Use a five-whys
analysis to identify the most likely root cause. Then tell me: is
this a structural problem, a skills problem, or a prioritization
problem? And what is the one intervention that would have the
highest impact if your diagnosis is correct?"

7. Generate Alternatives When You're Stuck
"I've been trying to solve [problem] by [current approach] and it's
not working. Generate five genuinely different alternative
approaches, specifically ones that challenge the assumption that
[state your core assumption]. For each one, tell me what it
requires that we don't currently have."
8. Identify the Real Bottleneck
"Here is the system or process I'm trying to improve: [describe].
Identify the single bottleneck that, if removed, would most
improve throughput. Then tell me why this bottleneck is often the
last one people look at."
9. Apply First Principles Thinking
"Everyone in [industry/domain] assumes that [describe a common
assumption]. Apply first principles thinking: break this
assumption down into its component parts and tell me which parts
are actually constraints and which parts are just habits. What
would you do differently if you were starting from scratch with no
inherited assumptions?"
10. Diagnose Why Something Isn't Working
"Here is what I was expecting to happen: [describe]. Here is what
actually happened: [describe]. Diagnose the most likely reasons
for the gap, not the obvious ones, but the ones that are hardest
to see from inside the situation. Tell me whether this looks like
a planning failure, an execution failure, or an assumption
failure."

When you need structured thinking, not just analysis.
11. Build a Decision Matrix
"I need to decide between [list options] using these criteria:
[list criteria]. Build a decision matrix that scores each
option against each criterion on a scale of 1-5. Weight the
criteria by importance to our current situation: [describe
priorities]. Give me the result and tell me which criterion
most influenced the outcome."

12. Run a Pre-Mortem
"I am about to commit to [decision or project]. Imagine it is 12
months from now and this has failed badly. Working backwards, what
are the three most likely reasons it failed? For each one, what
should I put in place now to reduce the probability?"
13. Apply Devil's Advocate
"My team has converged on the following decision: [describe]. Play
devil's advocate as rigorously as possible. Make the strongest case
against this decision, not a balanced view, the strongest possible
critique. Don't soften it. Then tell me what evidence would be most
likely to convince you the decision is actually right."
14. Analyze Second-Order Consequences
"If [decision], what happens next? Map the first-order consequences.
Then, for the two most significant first-order consequences, map the
second-order consequences, what happens as a result of those? Flag
the second-order consequence I'm least likely to have considered."

17. Navigate a Stakeholder Disagreement
"Here is a disagreement I'm navigating between [stakeholder A] and
[stakeholder B] about [issue]. Each party's position is: [describe].
What is the underlying interest beneath each stated position? Where
is there a potential zone of agreement they may not have seen? And
what is the minimum viable outcome that both parties could live
with?"
15. Worst-Case Scenario Planning
"I am considering [decision]. Walk me through the worst-case
scenario in detail, not the unlikely catastrophic one, but the
realistic bad outcome I should be prepared for. Tell me: how bad is
it really? Is it recoverable? And what would I need to have in place
to recover from it?"
18. Decide on Organizational Structure
"I'm considering reorganizing [describe current structure] to
[describe proposed structure]. Evaluate the proposal on: what it
optimizes for, what it sacrifices, who is likely to resist it and
why, and what would need to be true for it to work. Tell me the one
thing I'm most likely underestimating about this change."

Our most important outcome in the next [timeframe] is [describe].
Build a priority stack that clearly names what we're doing first,
what we're doing second, and what we're explicitly not doing. Include
one sentence of rationale for each that I can share with the team."

Decisions about how to allocate resources to grow faster or more efficiently.
21. Hire vs. Outsource
"I need [capability] for [purpose]. Evaluate three options: hiring
someone internally, outsourcing to an agency or contractor, or using
AI to partially or fully address the need. For each option, give me
the realistic cost, the timeline to full capability, and the main
risk."

22. Pricing Decision Framework
"I'm considering [pricing change: increase/restructure/add tier]. My
current pricing is [describe]. Walk me through: the most likely
customer response, the impact on new customer acquisition versus
retention, the message it sends about where we're positioned, and
the one unintended consequence I'm least likely to have considered."


23. Analyze Customer Feedback for Growth Signals
"Here is customer feedback I've collected: [paste]. Analyze it for
growth signals, not just the satisfaction data, but what it reveals
about: the customer segments most likely to expand, the use cases
we're underdeveloped in, and the competitor we're most often
replacing or being compared to. Give me the one growth hypothesis
this data most supports."
"I'm considering investing [describe resource] in [describe
opportunity]. Evaluate the investment on: expected return and time
to see it, key assumptions that must hold for the return to
materialize, what the minimum viable version of this investment
looks like, and at what point I should cut my losses if it's not
working."
24. Investment Decision Analysis
"I'm deciding whether to [enter/exit] [market or segment]. Here is
what I know about it: [describe]. Here is our current position:
[describe]. Evaluate the decision on: the strength of our right to
win, the opportunity cost of the resources required, and the
reversibility of the decision. Tell me the decision I'll regret more
in three years: entering too late, or exiting too early."
