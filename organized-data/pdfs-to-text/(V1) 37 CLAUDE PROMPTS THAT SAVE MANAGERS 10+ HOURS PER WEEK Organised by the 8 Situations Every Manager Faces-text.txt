SITUATION 1
When you need to send a message that actually lands.
The most common communication failure isn't the wrong message, it's the right
message in the wrong order. AI catches those structural problems before they reach
your team.
Prompt #21: All-Hands Talking Points
I am preparing for an all-hands meeting. The main topic is
[describe the topic]. Draft talking points that: open with the
headline message directly, explain the context and reasoning
in plain language, anticipate the three questions employees
are most likely to have and address them proactively, and
close with a clear call to action or next step.
TEAM COMMUNICATION
Pro tip: After drafting, ask "What question am I most likely to get that I have not
addressed?" This is almost always the most important thing to prepare for.

Prompt #72: Performance Review Preparation
I need to deliver a performance review for [role/level]. The
employee's performance has been [strong/mixed/below
expectations] and the key themes are [describe]. Help me
structure the review conversation so that it: leads with
genuine recognition before development areas, delivers
critical feedback using the situation-behavior- impact
framework, is specific enough to be actionable, and ends with
agreed next steps and support commitments.
Pro tip: Ask it to convert any general feedback you have into a specific SBI-format
example. Vague performance feedback is the most common reason development
conversations fail.

SITUATION 3
When you need to keep work moving without chaos.
The highest-leverage use of AI for managers isn't drafting emails. It is thinking
through operational problems before they become real ones. Risk assessment before
a project starts. Process redesign before the team hits the wall.
PROJECT AND OPERATIONS
Prompt #46: Project Risk Assessment
I am about to kick off [project name and description]. Before
we start, help me build a project risk assessment. Identify
the ten most likely risks across the categories of scope,
resources, timeline, stakeholder alignment, and external
dependencies. For each risk: probability, potential impact,
and one specific mitigation action. Flag the top three risks
that could derail the project entirely.
Pro tip: Run this at the start of every major project, not after it has already begun to
go wrong. Preventative risk management is ten times cheaper than reactive
recovery.
Pro tip: Ask "Critique my opening statement and tell me if I am burying the lead."
Difficult conversations go wrong most often in the first 30 seconds.
Also in this situation: Prompt #23 (Feedback Conversation Prep), Prompt #80
(Leadership Development Plan)

Prompt #48: KPI Framework Design
I need to design a KPI framework for [team/function]. The
framework should cover: two or three leading indicators that
predict future performance, two or three lagging indicators
that confirm results, and one or two health metrics that
prevent us from chasing top-line performance at the cost of
team wellbeing. For each KPI: how it is measured, the data
source, the reporting cadence, and what a good versus
concerning result looks like.

SITUATION 4
When meetings are eating the week and producing nothing.
The two most valuable meeting prompts aren't about running meetings better. They
are about deciding which meetings should not exist at all.
MEETING MANAGEMENT
Prompt #50: Meeting Effectiveness Audit
I am going to describe our current meeting culture and
structure. Identify: which meetings are likely providing less
value than their time cost, what meeting hygiene improvements
would have the most impact on team productivity, and what a
well-designed meeting architecture would look like for a team
of [our size and function]. Current state: [describe your
recurring meetings, their cadence, and the general state of
your meeting culture]
Pro tip: Most executive teams can reclaim 30 to 40% of meeting time without losing
meaningful collaboration. The hardest part is having the political conversation
about which meetings to eliminate.
Pro tip: If the team cannot remember all the KPIs, you have too many. Aim for five to
seven total.
Also in this situation: Prompt #43 (Process Bottleneck Diagnosis), Prompt #44
(Operations Efficiency Audit), Prompt #47 (Workflow Redesign Brief), Prompt #51
(OKR Design)

Prompt #54: Post-Mortem Framework
A project, initiative, or quarter did not go as planned. Help
me run a constructive post-mortem. Based on the following
context, identify: what we said would happen versus what
actually happened, the three root causes behind the gap, what
early warning signals we missed or ignored, and what we should
do specifically differently next time. Context: [describe what
happened]

SITUATION 5
When you are building the team and the process is slower than it needs to be.
HIRING AND TALENT
Prompt #76: Onboarding Program Design
Design a 30-60-90 day onboarding program for [role]. The
program should ensure that: by day 30 the new hire understands
the business, their role, and key relationships; by day 60
they are contributing independently; by day 90 they have clear
goals and are operating at full pace. For each phase: the
three to five most important activities, the key relationships
to establish, and how success is defined.
Pro tip: Ask "What is the single most important thing this person needs to
understand in their first 30 days that is not in any document?" The answer is
usually the most valuable part of the onboarding plan.
Pro tip: Ask "Was there a moment when we knew this was going wrong but did not
act?" Post-mortems are most useful when they surface the decision point, not just
the outcome.
Also in this situation: Prompt #16 (Board Update Structure), Prompt #49
(Capacity Planning Analysis)

individual manager quality, the segment of employees most at
risk, and the two actions with the highest potential impact.

SITUATION 6
When you need to think more clearly before committing.
STRATEGY AND PLANNING
Prompt #5: Assumptions Stress Test
I am going to share a strategy or plan. Identify every
assumption embedded in it, stated or unstated. Then rate each
assumption on two axes: how confident we should be that it is
true, and how damaging it would be to the strategy if it
turned out to be wrong. Flag the two or three assumptions that
pose the highest combined risk. Strategy: [describe your
current plan]
Pro tip: This is the single most useful prompt for any strategic planning process.
Run it on every major initiative before you present to leadership.
Pro tip: Ask "What are we doing well that we risk losing if we focus all our attention
on the gaps?" High-score items in engagement surveys are as important as low-
score items.
Also in this situation: Prompt #74 (Organisational Design Review), Prompt #77
(Succession Planning Framework)

Prompt #6: Three-Scenario Planning
For [this strategic initiative or decision], help me build
three planning scenarios: a base case, an optimistic case, and
a pessimistic case. For each scenario, describe the key
conditions that would make it true, the likely business
outcomes, and the three most important actions leadership
should take to either create or respond to that scenario.

SITUATION 7
When you need to turn numbers into a story that moves people.
DATA AND REPORTING
Prompt #84: Research Synthesis
I have gathered the following research or data on [topic].
Synthesize it into a briefing of no more than one page
covering: the three most important findings, what these
findings mean for our business specifically, the key areas of
uncertainty or conflicting evidence, and the one or two
questions that still need to be answered before we can act.
Research: [paste in your data or notes]
Pro tip: Ask "For each finding, tell me how confident we should be in it and what the
source quality is." The most valuable synthesis separates what is known from what
is assumed.
Pro tip: After generating the scenarios, ask "What early warning signals would tell
us which scenario is unfolding?" This turns the exercise into something you can
actually monitor.
Also in this situation: Prompt #4 (Strategic Options Prioritization), Prompt #92
(Decision Matrix Construction)

SITUATION 8
When you need to shift how the team operates, not just what it does.
CULTURE AND CHANGE
Prompt #75: Culture Assessment
Help me assess the current state of our organisational
culture. Based on the following context and data, identify:
the two or three strongest cultural attributes that define how
we operate at our best, the two or three cultural gaps between
our stated values and daily reality, the specific behaviors
reinforcing the gaps, and two concrete actions leadership
could take to close the most important gap. Context: [describe
your stated values, recent engagement data, and your
observations]
Pro tip: Culture is changed through behavior, not communication alone. For each
gap, ask "What specific behavior from leadership is the biggest driver of this gap?"
Pro tip: Data presentations that lead with methodology lose audiences. Always lead
with the conclusion and use the data to support it, not the reverse.
Also in this situation: Prompt #82 (Customer Feedback Synthesis), Prompt #83
(Survey Design), Prompt #88 (Qualitative Interview Analysis), Prompt #90
(Benchmarking Framework)

symptoms]. Help me diagnose the root cause by identifying:
whether this looks more like a structural, interpersonal, or
skills issue, the two or three questions I should ask to
confirm my diagnosis, and the intervention that would have the
highest impact if my diagnosis is correct. Team context:
[describe size, structure, and how long these challenges have
been present]
